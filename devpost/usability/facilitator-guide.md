# Formative student trial

Status: prepared, not conducted. No participant results or usability claims exist yet. This technical guide is not submission prose or video narration.

## Purpose and participants

Observe 3–5 adult students using one notice without coaching. Test whether they can find missed obligations, handle uncertainty and produce a trustworthy reviewed receipt. This is a small formative trial, not a representative study. Do not invent participant comments or results.

Use the public repository's `release/action-receipt.html`, downloaded and opened in a current desktop browser. It works offline. The owner-private hosted site is not a participant access route. No API key, account or payment is needed.

## First-use session, approximately 10–15 minutes

1. Assign one notice from `participant-notices.md`; rotate A, B, C across participants. Do not reveal this answer guide or demonstrate the queue first.
2. Ask the participant to read the task instructions, paste the notice, review it and produce the two files. Let them explain what they expect as they work. Start timing when they begin the app task.
3. Observe quietly. Record missed obligations, invented dates, misread conditions, confusing labels, help requested, and whether they inspect the files. If they request help, record the request and what assistance you provided. Do not count the assisted portion as independent completion.
4. Stop timing when they finish or choose to stop. Inspect the actual files against the notice. Record what happened, not just whether they liked the app.
5. Ask what felt unclear and what they would change. Keep their words separate from your interpretation. Then ask them to save and reopen a review and describe what happens to approvals and decisions. Record this separately from first-use timing.

Use anonymous participant codes. Do not put identities, private notices or recordings into the public repository. Recording is optional and requires the participant's agreement; written observations are sufficient.

A checklist may retain unresolved items with their warnings. Calendar entries require both approval and complete confirmed dates, times and offsets when applicable. A source decision alone must not create or approve a task, infer a deadline or certify completeness. Optional/conditional tasks may be omitted with the applicability decision recorded. Do not grade participants for choosing a defensible conservative interpretation.

## What to assess

- Did the person repair the unsupported consent/signature/waiver instruction without being taught the feature?
- Did they preserve uncertainty instead of guessing a deadline?
- Did the next-step panel help them find the relevant passage or action? Could they continue with approved checklist items while clarification was still pending? Record observed behavior, not an assumed improvement.
- Did they understand the difference between recording a source decision, approving an action and confirming a date?
- Do the saved and exported files match their intended decisions, including omissions, conditions and warnings?
- Could they recover from a mistake and understand why an edited or imported decision needs rechecking?

Use `observation-template.md` to record the real session. Investigate recurring confusion before adding features. Preserve failures and retest affected behavior. Completion time by itself does not establish time saved; do not claim a speed improvement without a fair manual-comparison task. Report small-trial counts plainly, with sample size and assistance disclosed. Findings and quotes must come from actual participants.

## Demo preparation after the trial

Use an explicitly invented notice to show the working sequence: fresh local scan, repair an unsupported obligation, record a source decision, approve the new item, keep the conflicting deadline out of the calendar, and inspect the final file. Choose the path based on actual trial observations. The entrant writes and speaks their own narration. Do not use this guide as a claimed transcript, testimonial or completed user study.
